\begin{abstract}
\noindent
Version~1 of this work proposed DDBM, a screening test that quantizes a
rank-normalized scalar series onto an integer lattice, builds a modular phase
from a cubic kernel, and reads departures from uniformity as evidence of
deterministic structure. This version keeps the goal, discards most of the
construction, and delivers what survives as a validated procedure.

\emph{What we removed.} The cubic kernel turns out to be one member of a
cyclotomic family: $S_p(N)=(N+1)^p-N^p$ factors into homogeneous cyclotomic
polynomials at consecutive integers, so $(N{+}1)/N$ is an exact $p$-th root of
unity modulo $S_p$ and the phase lattice has denominator $\Phi_n(1)$. The
identity is correct and, to our knowledge, previously unstated in this context,
but it buys nothing: because $\Xph$ is a deterministic function of the pair
$(N_t,\Delta N_t)$, the phase map is a lossy projection, and a direct occupancy
count on that pair scores $35/40$ against $34/40$ for the phases on our labeled
benchmark. The modular arithmetic is discarded.

\emph{What we found.} The order of operations decides whether the test is valid
at all. Detrending, AR(1) prewhitening and volatility standardization applied
\emph{before} rank normalization destroy distribution-freeness: empirical size
at nominal $0.05$ reaches $0.19$ for a lognormal marginal and $0.88$ for a
Cauchy marginal. Working on ranks alone restores exact distribution-freeness,
with a one-line proof, and returns nominal size across six marginals. We also
audit the surrogate stage, which is where every verdict ultimately rests: it
rejects a static monotone transform of a linear process---inside its own
null---in $25\%$ of replicates, and heteroscedastic processes in $50$--$75\%$.
That measurement invalidates the financial conclusion of v1 and of our own
earlier draft.

\emph{What we recommend.} A preprocessing-free, rank-based test with exact
Monte-Carlo calibration of the full scan, IAAFT confirmation, and documented
confound controls. Under matched conditions the best discriminating statistic
is the classical missing-ordinal-pattern count with the pattern length scaled to
the sample ($37/40$); pair occupancy follows at $35/40$. We claim no new
detection principle: the mechanism is the forbidden/missing-pattern paradigm and
coarse-grained transition statistics. The contribution is the calibrated
decision layer, the measured limits, and an open benchmark of 165 series whose
synthetic labels are computed from Lyapunov exponents rather than asserted.
\end{abstract}

\section{What this version claims, and what it withdraws}

Version~1 claimed a new detector: a modular-arithmetic phase statistic reaching
$92.5\%$ accuracy on a 40-system benchmark, ``without phase-space
reconstruction or training data''. Three of those claims do not survive scrutiny.

\paragraph{The method is not embedding-free.} The phase $\Xph_t$ is a function of
the pair $(N_t,N_{t+1})$, i.e.\ of a two-dimensional delay vector with $m=2$,
$\tau=1$, quantized at resolution $1/K$. Scanning $K$ is a scan over box scale,
and the count of realized pairs is a single-scale box count in that embedding.
Embedding parameters were not eliminated; they were fixed and hidden. The
statement in v1 is withdrawn.

\paragraph{The stated mechanism contradicts the preprocessing.} Version~1
attributed the signal to a singular marginal measure occupying few of $K$ bins.
Rank normalization removes exactly that: for a continuous marginal and
$n\gg K$, every bin receives about $n/K$ points regardless of how singular the
underlying measure is. Whatever the test detects, it is not marginal sparsity;
it is the geometry of \emph{transitions}. This reinterpretation is forced, and
it is what the present version analyses.

\paragraph{The $p$-values were not valid.} Phases from overlapping pairs are
serially dependent, while the two-sample Kolmogorov--Smirnov test used to score
them assumes independence; a Bonferroni correction over the scanned $K$ does not
repair a miscalibrated individual test. Section~\ref{sec:pivot} shows the defect
is worse than a dependence artifact: with the published order of operations the
procedure is not even distribution-free.

We also state plainly what is \emph{not} new. Discriminating determinism from
noise by the occupancy of symbol transitions is classical: symbolic dynamics and
forbidden words \cite{crutchfield}, the Bandt--Pompe ordinal
symbolization~\cite{bandtpompe}, forbidden and missing ordinal
patterns~\cite{amigo,zanin}, ordinal transition networks~\cite{mccullough}, and
direct determinism tests~\cite{kaplanglass,sugihara}. The confound analysed in
Section~\ref{sec:confounds} --- smooth stochastic processes imitating
low-dimensional determinism --- has been known since \cite{theiler1986,osborne}.
Nothing in this paper is a new detection principle.

\section{The cyclotomic identity, and why it does not help}
\label{sec:cyclo}

\subsection{The identity}

With $S_p(N)=(N+1)^p-N^p$ we have, by construction, $(N+1)^p\equiv N^p
\pmod{S_p(N)}$.

\begin{lemma}[order of the consecutive ratio]
\label{lem:order}
Let $N\ge 1$ and $p\ge 2$. Then $\gcd(N,S_p(N))=1$, and
$\rho=(N+1)N^{-1}\bmod S_p(N)$ has multiplicative order exactly $p$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Expanding, $S_p(N)=\sum_{j<p}\binom{p}{j}N^{j}\equiv 1 \pmod N$, so $N$ is
invertible. From $(N+1)^p\equiv N^p$ we get $\rho^{\,p}\equiv 1$, so the order
divides $p$. If the order were $d<p$ with $d\mid p$, then $S_p(N)$ would divide
$(N+1)^d-N^d$, which is a positive integer strictly smaller than $S_p(N)$ for
$N\ge 1$ and $d<p$ --- impossible. Hence the order is $p$.
\end{proof}

\noindent
The degenerate cell $N=0$ must be excluded: $S_p(0)=1$, the residue ring is
trivial and $N^{-1}$ does not exist. Implementations should either drop that
cell or map it to a fixed phase; v1 did neither.

Expanding the difference of powers gives the homogeneous cyclotomic
factorization
\begin{equation}
S_p(N)=\sum_{j=0}^{p-1}(N+1)^{j}N^{\,p-1-j}
=\prod_{d\mid p,\ d>1}\Phi_d(N+1,\,N),
\label{eq:cyclo}
\end{equation}
verified symbolically for $p=2,\dots,6$; e.g.\ $S_4=(2N{+}1)(2N^2{+}2N{+}1)
=\Phi_2\Phi_4$ and $S_6=(2N{+}1)(N^2{+}N{+}1)(3N^2{+}3N{+}1)=\Phi_2\Phi_6\Phi_3$.
Because the arguments are coprime, the classical primitive-divisor theory of
Bang and Zsigmondy~\cite{zsigmondy} applies: every prime divisor $q$ of
$\Phi_p(a,b)$ satisfies $q\equiv 1 \pmod p$ except possibly $q=p$. We verified
no violations for $p\in\{3,5,7\}$, $N\in[2,200]$. Consequently $p$ divides
$|(\mathbb{Z}/S_p)^\times|$ and an element of order $p$ must exist ---
Lemma~\ref{lem:order} is not an accident of small cases.

For $p=3$ the identity $3N^3=S_3(N)(N-1)+(2N+1)$ places the phases at
$\Xph(N,0)=k/3+O(1/N)$ with $k$ determined by $N\bmod 3$; the median distance to
$\{0,\tfrac13,\tfrac23\}$ is $2.2\times 10^{-4}$ over $N\in[2,2000]$. More
generally the lattice denominator equals $\Phi_n(1)$, which is $p$ for
$n=p^k$ and $1$ otherwise; measured denominators match for $n=2,\dots,12$.

We emphasise the scope: this is proved for $\Delta N=0$, and for the special
branch of the tent map where $\Delta N=N$ yields the exact resonance
$\Xph=(N{+}1)/(2N{+}1)\to\tfrac12$. That an \emph{arbitrary} deterministic
trajectory concentrates on the lattice more strongly than a stochastic one is an
empirical observation (Fig.~\ref{fig:mech}), not a theorem.

\subsection{Why the arithmetic is nevertheless discarded}

$\Xph$ is a deterministic function of $(N_t,\Delta N_t)$. Any test on $\Xph$ is
therefore majorized by a test on the pair itself: the phase map can only lose
information. Empirically it does. Under a matched protocol
(Section~\ref{sec:protocol}, preprocessing-free), a plain count of distinct
realized pairs scores $35/40$ against $34/40$ for the calibrated cyclotomic
phase scan, and the classical ordinal baseline scores $37/40$
(Table~\ref{tab:compare}).

An earlier draft of this version reported that cyclotomic kernels give
``$5$--$8\times$ sharper'' $z$-scores than power kernels. That statement is
withdrawn: the quoted values ($z\approx 750$ against a null simulated with a few
hundred replicates) lie far outside the support of the simulated null and cannot
be read as sensitivity. At matched nominal size the phase statistic is the
\emph{least} sensitive of the three (Table~\ref{tab:classwise}).

\section{Operation order decides validity}
\label{sec:pivot}

\subsection{The defect}

The published pipeline applies OLS detrending, AR(1) prewhitening and (data
dependent) rolling-volatility standardization to raw amplitudes, and ranks only
afterwards. The ranks of the residual then depend on the unknown marginal $F$,
so the calibration cannot be exact. We measured it: calibrate under a Gaussian
null with $B=1000$ replicates at $n=10^4$, then test i.i.d.\ draws from six
continuous marginals.

\begin{tabular}{lrrrr}

\noindent
At nominal $5\%$ the test rejects i.i.d.\ Cauchy data $88\%$ of the time. The
cause is straightforward: for a marginal without finite moments the fitted trend
and the AR(1) coefficient are unstable, and their instability writes
marginal-dependent structure into the ranks of the residual.

\subsection{The remedy, and its proof}

\begin{proposition}[distribution-freeness]
\label{prop:pivot}
Let $x_1,\dots,x_n$ be i.i.d.\ from any continuous distribution $F$, and let $T$
be any statistic that depends on the sample only through the vector of ranks.
Then the distribution of $T$ does not depend on $F$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Continuity gives distinct values almost surely, and the rank vector of an
i.i.d.\ sample is uniform on the $n!$ permutations for every continuous $F$.
A statistic measurable with respect to the rank vector therefore has a
distribution determined by $n$ alone.
\end{proof}

\noindent
The consequence is that the Monte-Carlo null is exact only if \emph{every}
amplitude operation is removed or performed after ranking. We compared four
orders empirically (Table~\ref{tab:orders}).

\noindent
Only variant~C passes the uniformity test at every marginal. Its cost is that
trends and linear autocorrelation are no longer removed before screening, so the
surrogate stage must carry that load alone; Section~\ref{sec:results} shows it
does so imperfectly (AR(1) with $\phi=0.9$ becomes a false positive for the pair
statistic). We regard the trade as clear-cut nonetheless: a test whose size is
$0.85$ on heavy tails is not usable, whereas a test that loses one hard case is.

The preprocessing was in any case partly redundant. Removing a linear trend and
linear autocorrelation is exactly what the surrogate stage tests for; performing
it twice, once destructively, gains nothing.

\section{The procedure we recommend}
\label{sec:protocol}

\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*,nosep]
\item \textbf{Symbolize.} Rank-normalize to $[0,1]$ with average ranks for ties.
No detrending, no prewhitening, no volatility scaling.
\item \textbf{Screen.} Compute the statistic on a grid (lattice resolutions
$K\in\{100,200,400,800\}$ for occupancy; pattern lengths $d$ with $d!$
comparable to $n$ for ordinal patterns). Standardize each cell by a Monte-Carlo
null and take the maximum; obtain
$p=(1+\#\{T_{\text{null}}\ge T_{\text{obs}}\})/(1+B)$. The multiplicity of the
scan is inside the simulated null, so no Bonferroni correction is applied.
\item \textbf{Confirm.} If screening rejects, test against IAAFT
surrogates~\cite{schreiber}, which reproduce the periodogram and the marginal to
high but not exact numerical accuracy and therefore probe temporal ordering.
\item \textbf{Classify.} Confirmed structure that is extremely narrowband
(spectral concentration $>0.95$ or $|\mathrm{ACF}|>0.99$) is reported as
\emph{(quasi-)periodic}; otherwise as \emph{confirmed nonlinear}, subdivided by
transition occupancy into \emph{low-occupancy, compatible with low-dimensional
determinism} and \emph{high-occupancy, nonlinear stochastic}. We deliberately
avoid the label ``chaos'': no surrogate test can establish determinism
(Section~\ref{sec:confounds}).
\end{enumerate}

\paragraph{Choosing the pattern length is not optional.} For $n=10^4$ the
expected fraction of unobserved ordinal patterns under an i.i.d.\ null is
$(1-1/d!)^{\,n-d+1}$: $0.0000$ for $d\le 6$, $0.138$ for $d=7$, $0.781$ for
$d=8$, $0.973$ for $d=9$. At $d\le 6$ the statistic is identically zero under the
null, its variance vanishes, and any standardized score is meaningless. We
report this because an earlier draft of this paper used $d\in\{4,5,6\}$ at
$n=10^4$ and obtained an apparently excellent baseline from a degenerate
statistic. The trap is easy to fall into and, so far as we can see, is not
flagged in the tooling.

\section{Benchmark and results}
\label{sec:results}

\subsection{The benchmark}

165 series with provenance; 46 carry ground-truth labels. Synthetic labels are
\emph{computed}: maps by direct variational iteration, flows by Benettin
renormalization on the ODE with the analytic Jacobian~\cite{benettin}, giving
$\lambda=0.900$ (Lorenz, $\rho=28$), $0.083$ (R\"ossler, $c=5.7$), $0.425$ (Chua)
and negative values for the regular regimes. Stochastic and mixed cases are
labeled by construction, not by an exponent --- a distinction we now state
explicitly, since ``detector-independent ground truth'' overstated it.

Computing $\lambda$ corrected a label carried in the v1 manifest, and the
correction is delicate. At $r=3.57$ the logistic map has
$\lambda=0.01096$ with a bootstrap CI of $[0.0109,0.0111]$, so it is weakly
chaotic rather than ``Regular''. But $r=3.57$ lies \emph{above} the Feigenbaum
accumulation point $r_\infty=3.5699457$ (where $\lambda\approx 2\times10^{-5}$),
and at $r=3.5705$ one finds $\lambda=-0.0039$: the parameter sits in a region
riddled with periodic windows. We report the case as correctly labeled but
hypersensitive, and all three statistics miss it.

The 46 labeled series are not 46 independent systems: several are coordinates of
one attractor or members of a parameter family. Accuracy figures should be read
accordingly, and we report class-wise counts rather than a single number where
possible.

\subsection{Matched comparison}

All three statistics were run through the identical protocol of
Section~\ref{sec:protocol} --- same calibration, same surrogate stage, same
decision rule, same auxiliary criteria. Only the discriminating statistic
differs. Scoring is strict: the six \emph{Mixed} (chaos${+}$noise) cases are
excluded rather than counted as correct under either label, a rule inherited
from v1 that inflated the apparent score by six unloseable cases.

which given its cost is sufficient.

\section{Measured limits}
\label{sec:confounds}

\subsection{The surrogate stage leaks}

Every verdict rests on the surrogate stage, so its size must be measured rather
than assumed. Table~\ref{tab:iaaft} gives rejection rates at nominal $0.05$.

\noindent
A static monotone transform of a linear Gaussian process is inside the null by
construction, and is rejected in a quarter of replicates --- a known weakness of
amplitude-adjusted surrogates~\cite{kugiumtzis,schreiber2000}, here quantified
for this class of statistics. Heteroscedastic processes are rejected at
$50$--$75\%$, which is the decisive number for applications.

\subsection{Consequences for the applied claims}

\paragraph{The financial conclusion is withdrawn.} Both v1 and our earlier draft
read S\&P~500 log returns as carrying nonlinear structure. Given
Table~\ref{tab:iaaft}, that reading is unsupported: heteroscedastic series are
rejected by this stage at $50$--$75\%$ regardless of nonlinear dynamics, and
returns are heavy-tailed, where Table~\ref{tab:pivot} shows the published
pipeline was also miscalibrated. Testing financial returns requires a
model-based null (constrained GARCH surrogates), which we have not run. The
correct present statement is that the test is uninformative for this class.

\paragraph{The climate conclusion is softened.} Daily HadCET anomalies, monthly
Ni\~no3.4, PDO and AMO show no evidence of low-dimensional determinism
\emph{within this test}, consistent with a stochastic climate
model~\cite{hasselmann} and with the resolution of the 1980s attractor
controversy~\cite{nicolis,grassbergerclimate}. Absence of detection is not
evidence of absence. The methodological point stands independently: screening
alone flags most climate series as ``structured'', so a pipeline without the
surrogate stage would reproduce the error of that era.

\paragraph{The EEG gradient is confounded.} On the Bonn data~\cite{andrzejak} the
confirmed-nonlinear rate rises monotonically across classes ($1/20$, $0/20$,
$1/20$, $4/20$, $17/20$ from healthy to ictal). But classes A/B are scalp
recordings and C/D/E are intracranial, differing in modality and amplitude
resolution; the gradient tracks that as well as clinical severity. No clinical
claim is made.

\subsection{Confounds inherent to the approach}

\emph{Nonlinearity is not determinism.} GARCH volatility is nonlinear (the
surrogate stage correctly rejects) and smooth (transition occupancy $0.41$,
inside the deterministic range), so it is misclassified. A random walk sits at
$0.17$. This is the effect described in \cite{theiler1986,osborne} and it is a
limit of surrogate methodology, not a threshold to be tuned.

\emph{Instrumental quantization manufactures the signal.} RR intervals at
$128$\,Hz carry $50$--$79$ distinct values in $10^4$ points; coarse digitization
creates transition sparsity for free. Referencing the occupancy statistic
against a random permutation of the \emph{same values} cancels the value
multiset exactly and largely removes the artifact ($0.04$--$0.09$ before,
$0.26$--$0.63$ after). We report a low-resolution flag and decline to interpret
the HRV verdicts physiologically.

\emph{Densely sampled flows.} Most structure of a smooth oversampled flow lives
in the spectrum, which IAAFT reproduces, so the surrogate stage has little
power. Reducing the flow to successive local maxima repairs this on our six
flows, including the two that fail on the raw series. We flag this as an
in-sample result: the reduction was devised on the cases that motivated it and
has not been validated out of sample.

\section{What is new, what is not, and what remains}

\paragraph{Not new.} The detection principle: transition occupancy and
forbidden/missing patterns in symbolic dynamics
\cite{crutchfield,bandtpompe,amigo,mccullough,zanin}. The mathematics: the
cyclotomic factorization, the order of an element, and the primitive-divisor
congruence are classical~\cite{zsigmondy}. The smooth-stochastic confound
\cite{theiler1986,osborne}. Surrogate methodology~\cite{theiler1992,schreiber}.

\paragraph{New, as far as we can determine.}
(i)~The cyclotomic identification of the phase lattice, Eq.~\eqref{eq:cyclo} with
Lemma~\ref{lem:order} --- correct, and negative in consequence.
(ii)~The demonstration that operation order determines distribution-freeness for
this family of tests, with measured sizes up to $0.85$ and a remedy
(Proposition~\ref{prop:pivot}, Table~\ref{tab:orders}).
(iii)~Measured empirical sizes for three symbolic statistics across six
marginals, and a size audit of the IAAFT stage for this class
(Table~\ref{tab:iaaft}).
(iv)~The saturation trap in specifying the ordinal baseline at fixed $n$.
(v)~An open benchmark with computed rather than asserted synthetic labels.

\paragraph{Useful, and missing from current tooling.} Widely used packages supply
these statistics but not the inference around them: \texttt{ordpy}~\cite{ordpy}
computes permutation entropy, complexity--entropy planes, missing patterns and
ordinal networks, and provides no hypothesis test, no $p$-values, no surrogate
framework and no null calibration; surrogate generators exist separately and are
not wired to the statistics. The ordinal literature itself notes that raw
missing-pattern counts cannot be thresholded by inspection, since they arise from
correlated stochastic processes as well as from
determinism~\cite{zanin}. The gap is a decision layer, and that is what this
paper supplies and validates.

\paragraph{Not done.} Power curves over $n$ and SNR at fixed size, instead of
accuracy at a single $\alpha$; a frozen protocol evaluated on a held-out set of
generators (the thresholds and the kernel choice here were selected on the same
46 series); model-based surrogates for heteroscedastic data; out-of-sample
validation of the return-map reduction; several realizations per system with
cluster-aware uncertainty. These are the experiments we would require of this
paper as referees, and we flag their absence rather than work around it.

\section{Conclusion}

The construction proposed in v1 does not survive: it is a lossy projection of a
pair statistic that is itself a rediscovery of a classical mechanism, and at
matched nominal size it is the weakest of the three statistics we compared. The
cubic kernel has an exact cyclotomic explanation, which is satisfying and
useless.

What survives is a procedure and three measurements. Rank-only symbolization
makes the test exactly distribution-free, which the published pipeline was not;
the surrogate stage that carries every verdict leaks at rates we now quantify;
and the choice of pattern length silently destroys the standard baseline at
common sample sizes. Together with an open benchmark whose synthetic labels are
computed, these make a working, honestly bounded screening procedure --- which is
less than v1 claimed, and more than it delivered.
